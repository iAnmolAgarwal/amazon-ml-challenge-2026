## 2. Leave-one-country-out (LOCO)
Protocol:
- Sample = shared 10% sample; quick = its 2% subset (44,140 S1; 1.96M pairs).
- Decisions: `rk.decide` with `rival=None` in every arm, floor 0.6 unless stated.
- Target-country labels are used only to score.
- Paired rule by target fold.
- The 006 stage-2 features (`_F4`) are excluded: they come from a stage-1 model trained on both countries' labels, so they would leak.

### 2.1 Base: 006 stage-1 features (48), 5-fold, quick subset (`loco.py`, `locoeval.py`, `locodec.py`)
| arm | India F0.5 | US F0.5 | India self-est. − actual | US self-est. − actual | India k/S1 | India empty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p006 (reference, OOF) | 0.97186 | 0.97560 | +0.0072 | +0.0027 | 3.287 | 6.07% |
| in-country model (tgt) | 0.96522 | 0.96992 | +0.0130 | +0.0055 | 3.266 | 5.76% |
| both countries (all) | 0.96346 | 0.96914 | +0.0057 | +0.0007 | 3.254 | 5.75% |
| **other-country model (src)** | **0.87877** | **0.91999** | **+0.0811** | **+0.0429** | 3.544 | 3.33% |

- **Measured, unseen-country loss:** src vs tgt = **−0.0865 India, −0.0499 US** (mean −0.0645, SE 0.0007, 0/5 folds positive).
- It is about 40x the loss from dropping the other country from training: tgt vs all is +0.0012, PASS.
- **Overconfidence:** under transfer the model's self-estimate overshoots the truth by 0.043-0.081. France's own gap is 0.04-0.07 (self-estimate 0.9668 vs implied 0.90-0.93, §3.1). That is the same size, so France behaves like an unseen country.
- **Calibration of src:** US pairs at p in (0.8, 0.95] are true 71% of the time (in-country 88%). India pairs at p in (0.6, 0.8] are true 42% (in-country 68%).
- India-src over-emits: k 3.54 vs 3.27 per S1, p mass 3.72 vs 3.38 true.
- **Best decision for the held-out country** (floor × empty ratio grid; the in-country optimum is floor 0.5-0.6):

| floor | 0.5 | 0.6 | 0.7 | 0.8 | 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| src India | 0.8764 | 0.8788 | 0.8814 | 0.8874 | **0.8958** |
| src US | 0.9186 | 0.9200 | 0.9207 | 0.9228 | **0.9249** |

  A floor of 0.9 recovers +0.017 India and +0.005 US, about a fifth of the loss. The empty ratio adds at most 0.0026 at floor 0.6 and nothing at 0.9. The grid stops at 0.9; the recipe runs extend it to 0.98.

## 3. France label-free evidence (test; 259,452 S1, 1.43M pool, 13.1M candidates)
### 3.1 Self-estimated F0.5 (006 probabilities, real decision, Monte Carlo; `selfest.py`)
- Test self-estimates: US 0.9732, India 0.9730, **France 0.9668**.
- Train OOF (sample): US self-estimate 0.9784 vs actual 0.9759; India 0.9789 vs actual 0.9722.
- **Derived:** if US/India lose on test what their self-estimates predict, the LB implies France = 0.933. If they transfer at CV, France = 0.902. Either way, France's self-estimate minus its actual score is 0.04-0.07, the LOCO transfer size.

### 3.2 Why France is hard: street agreement carries no evidence (`featshift.py`, `city.py`, `addrspec.py stats`)
- **Street agreement:** France candidates rejected by 006 share the S1's street 77.5% of the time, against 15-20% for train negatives. Address token-set on rejected France candidates is 83.9, against 61-71 for train negatives.
- **Not a town problem:** among French same-street gray pairs (not emitted, p > 0.1), 8,481 are in the same town and 36 in a different town. So a city/commune feature is not the France fix. (In India, same-street different-town pairs are 48,761 negatives vs 182 positives, which may be worth testing in-country.)
- **Crowding:** France S1 on a house+street shared by other S1: IDF < 10 for 18.7% (India 16.1%, US 1.0%). France is India-like in address crowding and US-like in script.
- **Postal:** postal features are missing on 99.9% of pairs in every country, so they are not a France gap.

### 3.3 France overconfidence, label-free (`conflict.py`; a pool record matches ≤ 1 S1, so Σp over its S1 should be ≤ 1)
Excess Σmax(0, Σp−1) per S1:
- train OOF: US 0.0028, India 0.0015;
- test: US 0.0021, India 0.0014, **France 0.0090**.

Pool records claimed by ≥ 2 S1 at p > 0.2, per S1: France 0.056 vs 0.024-0.035. France also carries 3.68 probability mass per S1, against 3.49-3.52 for US/India test. In LOCO the source model doubles over-claiming (US 0.00016 → 0.00030, India 0.00013 → 0.00027 on the quick subset) and inflates the mass. This is the same signature France shows.

### 3.4 The French gray zone is the sibling-decoy operator (`frsample.py`, `frhouse.py`)
- **30 sampled S1:** the confusions are pool records on the same street and in the same town, house number shifted by a few, with the same name up to the legal form (41 → 42, 370 → 375, 47 → 52, 23 → 34, 3 → 4, all in r9's decoy shift set D).
- **Hidden conflicts:** one emitted decoy's pool house was dropped by the parser ("375 Crs De La Somre"), so the conflict was invisible to the model.
- **Emitted France pairs by house category:**
  - parsed: equal 81.7%, missing 16.5%, near ≤ 20 1.12%, prefix/suffix 0.23%, other 0.46%;
  - after recovering the raw first number: equal 86.6%, missing 10.6%, **near ≤ 20 1.92%**, prefix/suffix 0.27%, other 0.65%;
  - that is 0.098 conflicting pairs per S1, and 8.1% of France S1 have at least one.
- **Comparison with train gold:** near ≤ 20 is 1.56% of US gold and 2.7% of India gold. Prefix/suffix is 4.2% / 3.7% of gold, and 006 OOF precision on near ≤ 20 emitted pairs is only 0.87 (US).
- **Reading:** France has almost no prefix/suffix noise (15x less than US gold) yet a US-level near-shift share. So most French near-shift emissions are probably decoys (derived).

### 3.5 Parse defects (`frdefect.py`)
- **Raise uncertainty (gray rate 2.3% vs 1.3%):** house number dropped by the FR fallback regex (7.8% of pool records; 74k emitted pairs, 16.7k gray) and unmapped street types (ch, crs, q, psg, res, b, t, cour; 2.8% of pool).
- **No effect:** N°/Nº, et/&/+, EI/Ets/Sté/Cie/Société, accent-deleted web forms and CEDEX all show gray rates of 0.4-1.4% vs a 1.4% base.
