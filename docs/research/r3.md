**Mass calibration (label-free on the target).** Shift the held-out country's logits by one constant δ ≥ 0 so that its mean probability mass per S1 equals the true retrieved matches per S1 of the labeled source country: US 3.403, India 3.382 on the quick sample. Then apply the normal decision at floor 0.6. `lococal.py`, `lococal2.py`.
- **India held-out:** mass 3.61 against 3.40, δ = 1.65.
- **US held-out:** mass 3.22, below target, so δ = 0 (one-sided) and nothing changes.
- **Why it beats a fixed floor:** it strengthens the decision only where the model is over-claiming, so it never costs the calibrated direction.
- **Sensitivity:** gains fall monotonically as the target rises. Aiming 0.1 low is better and aiming 0.2 high is harmless. The recipe is robust to not knowing France's exact match rate.

## 4. France recipe: exact spec (France only, test time, label-free)
1. **Input:** the shipped model's final pair probabilities p for all test candidate pairs, **after the stage-3 correction and before `decide`**.
2. **Masses:** per country c, N_c = number of test S1 of c (S1 without candidates count, with Σp = 0), and M_c = Σ p over c's pairs / N_c. That is the mean over S1 of each S1's Σp.
3. **Target:** T = (Σp over US and India pairs) / (N_US + N_India), the S1-weighted US/India test mass of the same model. This mirrors LOCO, where the target is the labeled country's calibrated mass. The aggressive variant T − 0.1 gains +0.0015 more in LOCO but costs −0.0023 if France is already calibrated (§2.4).
4. **One-sided rule:** if M_France ≤ T, then δ = 0 and nothing changes. Otherwise find δ in (0, 6] by bisection (50 steps) on f(δ) = (1/N_France) Σ over France pairs of σ(logit(p) − δ) − T, which decreases in δ. Clip p to [1e-7, 1 − 1e-7] first.
5. **Apply:** p' = σ(logit(p) − δ) on France pairs only; US and India unchanged. Then run the usual decision: pool ownership, expected-F top-k, P(no match), floor 0.6. The shift is monotone and uniform within France, so ownership is unchanged. Only k, the empty choice and the floor move.
6. **Guards** (printed by `frcal.py`):
   - France emitted per S1 should fall toward US/India's (006: 3.448 vs 3.33), and the France empty rate should rise toward 5.6-5.9%.
   - Dropped France pairs should show a higher house-conflict share and more mid-range probabilities than kept pairs.
   - If δ = 0, 009 already fixed France calibration, and nothing is gained.
7. **Script:** `research/r3/frcal.py <test_probs.parquet with s1_idx, pool_idx, prob> <out.parquet> [usin | <T>]`. It needs norm/test_s1 and feat/test_feat, and runs in about 2 min at ≤ 8 GB. The logit shift was checked against numpy.

**Expected gain** (derived from §2.3, about 0.09 per unit of excess mass per S1):

| France excess (M_France − T) | 0.05 | 0.10 | 0.18 (006 level) |
|---|---|---|---|
| France F0.5 | +0.004 | +0.009 | +0.016 |
| LB | +0.0007 | +0.0014 | +0.0024 |

Readings:
- **France is recall-limited, not over-claiming** (D, assuming France shares the generator's t distribution, which US and India do exactly). It has 0.19 more gray owner pairs per S1 and 0.08-0.11 fewer confident pairs than US/India.
  - Counter-evidence (M): France has fewer pool records per S1, 5.53 vs US 5.76 and India 5.82 (train 4.68 for both).
  - If France had the US/India test orphan rate (≈ 2.33 per S1), its mean t would be 3.20, not 3.46, and its emission would be about right.
  - The identical US/India t histograms make a shared t more likely (G), but this is the key unverifiable assumption.
- **Where the gray mass sits:** about two thirds of France's extra unemitted mass is in pairs whose address differs, which is where decoy house shifts live. The rest is in same-address operators.
- **Same-address operators** are true-match noise in train (gold 0.89-0.999), and the model is sure of them in US/India. In France:
  - French filler swaps are emitted (club→groupe/services/france/developpement/fils/associes, 0.9-1.0).
  - Descriptor-for-descriptor swaps (Sportive → Club, Comite → Pharmacie) and invented brand names ("Clinique de Pierre" → "Avionyx") sit at p 0.2-0.6.
  - Whether these are true noise or French sibling decoys cannot be told without labels. 44% of the French gray pairs carry dm_swap > 0 (US 0.4%).
- **Some French words are dual-use** (D, from pool counts in r1 §2.6). The pure decoy words have near-equal pool counts: participations 34,279, holding 33,983, distribution 34,205, international 34,571.
  - groupe (53,799) and developpement (48,013) ≈ 34k (decoy) + ~10.6k (the filler count of associes) + base names.
  - So both words are used as decoy and as filler, and a pure "groupe" insertion is truly ambiguous. With p ~0.24 (G), leaving it out is right.
- **No label-free France lever remains with a CV-testable gain on 018.** Mass calibration is spent. A fixed floor, self-training and two-stage (e) fail in LOCO. The lexical layer already helps.
- **Next lead:** France-specific ranking of the same-address operators (descriptor swap, brand rename, dual-use insertion). Its ceiling is the extra unemitted mass, 0.03/S1 same-address plus about 0.06/S1 other. If all of it were recoverable, that is up to about +0.01-0.02 France, LB +0.0015-0.003 (G); realistically a fraction.
  - It needs a France proxy with labels, e.g. a synthetic French relabelling of train or a hand-labelled sample of 300 gray pairs.
  - No LOCO direction can test French vocabulary.
